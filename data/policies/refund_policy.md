## Purpose
This policy defines when a customer may be eligible for a refund, how refund requests are evaluated, and which support actions are allowed before approval is granted. The policy promotes consistency across customer-support teams, reduces disputes, and ensures that exceptions are handled with appropriate oversight.

## Scope
This policy applies to refund requests for service fees, subscription charges, one-time purchases, failed deliveries or service activation issues, and cases where an account or product was not delivered as promised. It covers all customer-support channels, including voice, chat, email, and self-service escalations.

## Procedure
1. Collect the customer’s account details, service order or invoice number, and the reason for the refund request.
2. Confirm whether the issue is within the refund eligibility window, the service category, and any exclusion rules listed in the customer agreement or product terms.
3. Review whether the customer has already received a partial credit, account adjustment, or prior refund for the same incident.
4. Assess whether the root cause is customer error, provider issue, product defect, billing error, or a service outage affecting the promised deliverable.
5. If the claim qualifies, route the case to the refund approver for the amount and method of reimbursement. Approved refund methods may include credit to the original payment method, account credit, or voucher credit according to policy and customer preference.
6. If the request is denied, provide a clear explanation tied to the eligibility criteria and outline any alternative resolution options available to the customer.
7. Document the customer communication, approval decision, refund reference number, and associated timeline in the case record.
8. If the refund is delayed beyond the standard processing window, notify the customer with the expected time frame and provide the next support contact point.

## Exceptions
Exceptions to standard eligibility rules may be approved only in these circumstances:
- a system failure or service outage prevented the customer from using the product or service even though the billing was processed;
- a duplicate charge, failed authorization, or misapplied invoice created an incorrect financial result;
- a customer was subjected to a temporary service issue that materially reduced value after the subscription began;
- a documented goodwill adjustment is approved by the Customer Resolution Manager.

All exceptions must be approved, reason-coded, and logged in the refund ledger to ensure transparency and future auditability.

## Escalation guidance
Escalate to Finance Operations when:
- the refund amount exceeds the approval threshold for the support team;
- the original payment method is unavailable or a refund cannot be issued via the standard channel;
- multiple charges or duplicate invoice errors appear related to the same order;
- a charge dispute involves legal, compliance, or financial review concerns.

Escalate to the Customer Resolution Manager when the customer disputes a denial, alleges unfair treatment, or requests a policy exception not clearly covered by the standard criteria. Include the case ID, service details, refund rationale, prior customer communications, and any supporting evidence so the approver can make a full decision without re-asking the customer for duplicate information.
